01 Stem

A banana plant fruits once. Then its stem is cut down — and is usually discarded or burned.

3M+ tonnes a year

Banana is one of Rwanda’s most abundant crops. By RE-BANATEX’s estimate, more than three million tonnes of banana trunks are discarded or burned in the country every year.†

That residue is our raw material. We buy stems and leaves from local farmers after the fruit harvest, so they earn extra income from the same crop.

02 Fibre

Inside every stem, a long natural fibre.

The fibre was always there; getting it out takes the right machine. RE-BANATEX founder Jonathan Shauri, a mechanical engineer, designed the company’s first fibre extraction machine, and work on its processing machines began in October 2022.

What comes out is a natural fibre that RE-BANATEX spins into yarn, blends with cotton, weaves into cloth and makes into vegan leather. Biodegradable, and grown in Rwanda.

04 Process

Seven phases from stem to cloth.

RE-BANATEX’s own production method, from the plantation to the loom.

01/07
  1. 01

    Raw material

    Banana plantations across Rwanda. Each plant fruits once; once the bunch is harvested, its stem is left behind.

  2. 02

    Collection

    Stems and leaves are bought from local farmers after the fruit harvest — income from material that would otherwise be discarded or burned.

  3. 03

    Cutting

    Stems are cut and prepared for extraction.

  4. 04

    Extraction

    Custom-built machines separate the long fibres from the rest of the stem.

  5. 05

    Treatment

    The fibre is processed and dyed, ready to be spun.

  6. 06

    Spinning

    Fibre becomes RE-BANATEX yarn — including blends with cotton for a soft, durable textile.

  7. 07

    Weaving

    On handlooms and specialised machines, yarn becomes fabric — and fabric becomes finished products.

07 Origin

Rooted in Rwanda. Built for wider markets.

RE-BANATEX is set up as a system in which farmers, engineers and designers work on the same material:

  1. A Farmers supply the stems
  2. B Engineers build the machines
  3. C Makers spin, weave and finish
  4. D Designers turn material into products

“The reason why RE-BANATEX is implementing this project is to see how we can support the ‘Made in Rwanda’ movement with locally available material.”

Jonathan Shauri Founder & CEO. Mechanical engineer; designed RE-BANATEX’s first fibre extraction machine.
